## Answer

Ticket 09's share of C2 is built, reviewed and on `main`, and with it every C2 ticket, 05 to 09, is resolved. In `internal/core/workspace`, the owner approves one exact field law, validation policy and set of workspace-local policies by their digests; Core checks and loads them, publishes their bytes and pins them for the life of the log, and records the request with an immediate or not-before schedule. One request may be pending per colony. Replacing or cancelling it names its revision; a request repeated under its ID changes nothing, and a changed one, a stale revision, a stale expected manifest, or a past or ambiguous time is refused without writing anything. When the request is due, Core records a hold that refuses new finalization intents in the affected workspaces, waits until their earlier intents are resolved, and then records one colony-wide switch at the actual trusted time, never earlier; an untrusted clock holds instead of switching. After the first switch every new intent names the active manifest. After a restart, Open rebuilds the request, its hold and the active manifest from the log without reading the clock or writing, and fences the affected workspaces until an explicit resume; the switch happens at most once, and a cancellation removes only its own hold. Core writes F2-09's records as the owner froze them (D124). C2's product still admits nothing until C4: an activation retains check and environment bytes as identities only, these paths are reached only by the package's tests, and C3 authenticates callers. [B28](../../../docs/DECISIONS.md#b28-c2-one-off-policy-activation-ticket-09) records the build, the test-cost work the owner asked for during it, its amendments of tickets 07's and 08's entries and the reading of C2's gates. All of this is verified on Windows; the Linux and macOS rows first run in C3's CI.
