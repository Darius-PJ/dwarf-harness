## Answer

No product defect was found in `internal/core/winexec`: the product's Job-zero proof, Job accounting polled to zero active processes, is correct. In runs 40773519796 and 41133154184 the Go race runtime (ThreadSanitizer) failed its fixed-address allocation with error 487 at startup inside the sandboxed race-built payload. That is a toolchain-class cause outside the package, recorded and routed here by naming it; its finer trigger is not established, and the fix removes the tests' dependence on it. In run 42338417245 the test observer treated a native PID census taken during the Job's rundown as authoritative. `TestSeparateRunsCannotReadPeerInput` also had an ordering defect of its own, separate from its hosted failure: both peers were released at once and each run's directory is removed after its own tree exits, so a peer could probe a target already deleted and get error 3, as a local run did on 2026-10-27. A run of the old code with a skewed release and S2's rule break RB2 show it.

Three slices changed only the package's tests:
- S1 stages a plain, cgo-free companion build of the package's tests as the sandboxed payload in race-built test processes; the executor, its pumps and locks, and the supervisor children stay race-built. Each race-built test process builds the companion once (`-vet=off -race=false`) with the `go` command found on `PATH`, whose version must equal the test binary's; a mismatch or error fails the requesting test, with no fallback. Supervisors receive the companion's path as a fourth argument and never build, and `TestMain` removes the companion after the tests. The build time is fixture setup, reported apart from the native rows.
- S2 makes the peer test a rendezvous: each peer payload writes a marker after its four probes and then waits for its release; the test releases A, waits for A's marker, releases B and waits for B's marker, and requires both roots alive and both targets unchanged before either payload may exit. The marker and release files are private run-local test controls, not a product protocol.
- S3 proves native closure only at stable phases: a strict PID census while the root is suspended, at count-ready and in a held tree; Job accounting at zero at `tree_exited` and `closed_fence`; and no native query at the other, diagnostic phases. The same rule now covers the package's two other censuses after a root's exit: `liveHeldDescendant` still requires exactly one live, listed descendant but no longer the exited root's rundown counts, and `testOuterJob`'s cleanup proves zero active processes by polling Job accounting after termination. The supervisor's test frame gains two additive fields, `ObservedPhase` and `PIDListKnown`.

The ruling's amendment 3 supersedes ticket 45's ruling §8.1 (note 26), which had declined a build inside the tests, for this one package-local fixture only: a plain, cgo-free build of the same sources, cached once per process and cleaned by the existing `TestMain`. It authorizes no other build inside tests, and no decision entry records it, since no standing rule changes (D161).

The ruling's clarification 1 gives the product reading of the per-Job CPU limit. `UserCPU` is Windows' per-Job user-time limit (`JOB_OBJECT_LIMIT_JOB_TIME`, `PerJobUserTimeLimit`; `job_windows.go:34-36`). Windows checks the Job's aggregate user time periodically and, at the first check that finds it over the limit, terminates every process with `ERROR_NOT_ENOUGH_QUOTA` (Microsoft Learn, `JOBOBJECT_BASIC_LIMIT_INFORMATION`); the check interval is not documented. Termination therefore follows the configured time rather than meeting it: with a 2.0 s limit, the Job's user time at termination was 2.09–6.69 s in the 30 local passes on record, and under full local load two replicas (base c89447f: setup 13.1 s, user 2.09 s; F b9efa89: setup 8.8 s, user 4.48 s) reached the admission-anchored 20 s `Wall` first, where the executor reported `TimedOut` with `TreeExited` and `ResourcesReusable` true. `UserCPU` bounds the tree's aggregate CPU to the limit plus the accrual of one check interval; `Wall` and `Deadline` remain the hard bounds. This is Windows' documented behaviour, not a product defect; no constant was changed; the hosted margin is unmeasured (the race step runs without `-v`) and is evidenced by the series' twenty rows and the hosted history.

Both full-load replicas of the check's race step were samples, never acceptance. The base replica on c89447f reproduced no startup failure and had two winexec timing failures under a load heavier than the hosted step's: the CPU row reached its `Wall`, and a supervisor's handshake guard expired. The replica on F had one, `TestNativeUserCPULimit`, recorded as a failed sample under clarification 1, not relabelled a pass. No constant was changed and no guard was sized from either. The series' twenty counted runs are criterion 5's evidence, not a guarantee that flakes are absent.

Limits: the hosted overshoot of the per-Job user-time check stays unmeasured. A one-sentence Go comment stating the reading above is deferred to the next ticket that edits a winexec production file, because any Go edit during this ticket would have made a new final commit and a new series. The ruling's third full-load replica was not spent. The finer trigger of the race runtime's startup failure and the native trigger of the hosted census pair remain unestablished. This ticket gives no C10 exit, A2 qualification or capacity proof.
