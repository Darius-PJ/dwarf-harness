## Answer

Core now holds an agent's ordinary work, or requires a declared first action, behind one generic eligibility guard that only the owner sets and releases; C3 knows nothing of C8's lifecycle. The work followed a fresh Fable ruling with twelve amendments and no owner gate, in four slices.

S1 froze F3-90 (D174): two version-1 `runtime.` records, an eligibility change and its receipt, which embeds the schema-8 canonical submission (the F3-90 catalogue in [`docs/CONTRACTS.md`](../../../docs/CONTRACTS.md) and the F3-90 section of the [`contract/ledger` documentation](../../../contract/ledger/doc.go)). An agent is `eligible`, `ordinary_work_ineligible` or `first_action_required`, with an open reason and a held operation identity.

S2 built the hold and release in `internal/core/agents`: the owner's `HoldOrdinaryWork` and `ReleaseEligibility` through Core's trusted Service seam; one guard, after ordinary authorization, at every fence in producer preflight and in replay (Session and native turn starts, `task.start`, the scheduler, paid admission and dispatch, local admission and start, tool start and atomic effects); an inert restart in which `Open` resumes nothing and only the existing explicit reconciliation ends old work; a hold that is never a stop, a park or a busy answer, so a held turn continues once on release; the agent's state and its turns' held flags published together before a commit is visible; and status, turn, event and task-readiness views that show the held state and disclose the declaration only to current authority.

S3 added the declared first action: the owner's `RequireFirstAction` and `DeclareNextFirstAction`; the exact predeclared context, turn, start and admission command IDs, optional task binding and input digest, verified before native admission; the `loop.bind` identity fence; Core's record of a refused first start; and the first action's disposition in the views. It also fixed a gap before native admission, in which a concurrent same-command send rendered again and failed at `loop.bind`, and a Session cancel that could fail with `ErrContinuationStale` in ticket 16's owner code.

S4 added the terminal surface: `message.send` results, attach, and context and turn status render the held state and the first action's identity and disposition, and events render the held state and the declaration; its follow-up keeps derived first-action facts out of eligibility events, so status stays their source. Criterion 6 names the §3.1 smoke and the executable row. The [agents](../../../internal/core/agents/doc.go), [session](../../../internal/core/session/doc.go) and [terminal](../../../internal/core/terminal/doc.go) package documentation states the rules.

Each slice's landing SHA ran the full check on Windows, Linux and macOS before `main` moved (D153): S1's check 112 (run 44713385628) on 4cf6ecd and S2's check 114 (run 44759993683) on cc9cfcb succeeded on all three systems; S3's check 116 (run 44796197884) on 4b4ecfb succeeded on all three systems; and S4's check 117 (run 44804992603) on 54c1f79 succeeded on Windows, Linux and macOS, in both tests and race.

Later producers reach the same boundary: C6 message delivery and C7 view commands through Core's Session and native entrances, while C8's ticket 100 will release through the same owner transition, and a Core-originated hold for ticket 131 would be an additive operation at that ticket's own freeze (amendment 8). Those integrations remain their own gates. This makes no C3 Done claim: tickets 91 and 146 remain, and C3's status is stated only in its [Status line](../../../docs/ROADMAP-CORE.md#c3-agent-runtime) and [board row](../../../docs/ROADMAPS.md#status-board).
