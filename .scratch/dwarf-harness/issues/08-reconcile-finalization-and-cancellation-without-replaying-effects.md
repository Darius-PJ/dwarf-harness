## Answer

Ticket 08's share of C2 is built, reviewed and on `main`, and C2 stays In progress. In `internal/core/workspace`, Core finalizes a request's current clean generation in one serialized sequence per workspace: every Git read and the record-size check first, then the durable intent, one guarded compare-and-swap of `main`, the flushes that make it durable, the durable acceptance, and only then the request's slot. A moved `main` is not accepted work: until the acceptance is recorded the request is finalizing and the workspace blocks further finalization. A generation whose tree equals its accepted predecessor's ends no-change, with no intent and no update of `main`. The owner or the submitting context may cancel a request until its intent is durable, and afterwards cancellation is too late; a writer's end or a cancelled call cancels nothing. After a restart, a workspace with unfinished requests refuses new work until an explicit bookkeeping phase runs. Given proof that the earlier writers stopped, the phase records an unresolved intent as interrupted when `main` is still at the old tip, completes its acceptance without a second Git update when `main` is at the target, and blocks on anything else; it ends every other unfinished request interrupted, for its author to resubmit. Core writes F2-08's records as the owner froze them (D123). C2's product still admits nothing until C4: these paths are reached only by the package's tests in disposable repositories, and C3 authenticates callers; policy activation (ticket 09) remains. [B27](../../../docs/DECISIONS.md#b27-c2-finalization-cancellation-and-recovery-ticket-08) records the build, including its amendments of tickets 06's and 07's entries; Core Appendix A.14 records the cost of each step of a finalization, a no-change ending and a reconciliation. All of this is verified on Windows; the Linux and macOS rows first run in C3's CI.
